# Neus UI

## Overview

Neus is a clear, approachable interface for application forms,
navigation, and data workflows. Its identity combines Manrope, indigo emphasis,
pill-shaped controls, quiet content surfaces, and a distinctive curved sidebar.
Keep those signatures consistent as the library grows; decorative treatments
must not compete with the task. These are Neus UI project decisions.

Format: [Google DESIGN.md](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md/blob/main/docs/spec.md) (alpha; consulted 2026-10-15).

## Colors

Use indigo for primary actions and navigation, neutral surfaces
for content, and separate success/error/information roles for feedback. Keep
primary text distinct from supporting text and placeholders. Color must accompany
a label, icon, border, or other recognizable state cue. In dark mode, preserve
semantic roles while adjusting both foreground and background for legibility.
Orange marks warnings: states that need attention but are not errors.

## Typography

Use Manrope for interface text, with system sans-serif fallbacks;
reserve monospace for code and identifiers. Establish hierarchy through size,
weight, and spacing before color. Keep prose and field labels in natural case.
Use the spaced uppercase treatment only for short action and navigation labels.
Public pages open with one display headline and a lead; running text stays at
16px or more. The overline is the one uppercase text style.

## Layout

Build spacing from the shared four-pixel rhythm. Group related
fields closely and separate sections more generously. Align labels, inputs, and
actions; allow wrapping instead of shrinking text. Pages sit in a Container
(1120px by default) and are divided into Section bands: alternate the default and
subtle tones instead of drawing rules, and keep the accent tone for one band.

## Elevation & Depth

Separate ordinary content through spacing, subtle borders, and
surface contrast. Reserve shadows for floating controls and overlays. Keep focus
recognizable independently of elevation. Motion should explain a state change;
respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Shapes

Use pills for short actions and text-entry controls, rounded
rectangles for content, and the concave/convex curve only as a navigation signature.
Do not apply pill rounding to every container or copy navigation decoration into
form controls.

## Components

### Button

A solid button carries the main action; outlined and text actions
provide lower emphasis. Keep size independent of importance. Hover and press must
be visible, and keyboard focus must remain distinct. Loading must communicate
progress without losing the action's accessible name or causing disruptive resizing.

### Card

A Card groups related content; its children own actions. Keep
media inside the rounded surface and place spacing in Header, Content, and Footer.
Footer actions keep the content's side and bottom inset; a full-width action never
touches the card edge.
Do not make a passive surface look clickable through hover elevation.

### Input

Keep a visible label, legible value, subordinate placeholder,
and explicit error message. Focus must remain evident when invalid. A read-only
value stays readable and distinct from an unavailable field.

### Sidebar

Use the primary-colored panel to anchor navigation. Keep hover,
current destination, and keyboard focus distinguishable. Preserve readable labels
when a rail expands and keep the mobile close control discoverable.

### Hero

One display headline in Manrope bold, a lead in secondary ink, one solid pill action with its note beside or under it. Split with the real product or a picture; indigo stays on the action.

### Feature list

Cards with a tinted icon tile and a short title, three per row on desktop; a plain grid when the section already has a strong anchor.

### Testimonial

Cards with an initials avatar, the name and the role, so every quote reads with its author; a featured card for one strong quote. Never stars or ratings the brief did not give.

### Supporting controls and content

Use the same semantic foreground, surface and focus roles for selectors, date and time controls, feedback and data views. Keep selected values distinct from hover and keyboard focus. Floating panels share the overlay geometry; tables and calendars preserve readable alignment when space is constrained. Templates establish page rhythm and responsive grouping without changing the visual identity of embedded controls.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do check light/dark, nested scopes, keyboard focus, long content, and reduced motion.
- Do preserve Neus defaults at every provider boundary when adding a token.
- Don't use gradients, glows, or all-uppercase copy as a substitute for hierarchy.
- Do use overlines, icon tiles, one or two brand or inverse bands and pill actions on public pages; never gradients, glows or glass.
